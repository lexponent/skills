# Pleading class — research areas and output sections

Use this file when the filing initiates or responds to a civil action: complaints, cross-complaints, answers, amended pleadings, related accompanying documents.

## Research areas (in addition to common.md)

- First-page caption format for pleadings (typically differs from motions — no hearing date, no hearing department)
- Causes of action structure: rules governing how each cause of action must be separately stated, captioned, and numbered
- Allegations: jurisdictional, venue, factual; rules on how each cause of action incorporates prior allegations
- Prayer for relief: enumeration of damages, costs, attorney's fees, equitable relief
- Verification (where required by the jurisdiction): when, form, signature
- Demand for jury trial: timing and form (face of pleading or separate document)
- Statement of damages or amount in controversy (where separately required, e.g., for some categories of personal-injury actions)
- Fictitious-defendant pleadings (where the jurisdiction permits) and the procedure for adding/dismissing them
- Exhibits attached to and incorporated by reference into the pleading
- Accompanying documents (e.g., summons or equivalent issuing document for initiating pleadings; service-of-process requirements)
- Responsive-pleading deadlines and extensions by service method
- Amendment procedure: by right vs. by leave; relation-back rules

## Output sections (in addition to common.md)

- First Page / Caption (pleading-specific)
- Constituent Documents (varies by pleading type and jurisdiction)
- Causes of Action (numbering, captioning, allegations)
- Prayer for Relief
- Verification (conditional)
- Demand for Jury Trial (conditional)
- Statement of Damages (conditional)
- Fictitious-Defendant Allegations (conditional, where the jurisdiction permits)
- Service and Filing Deadlines (responsive pleading, where applicable)

These lists are research hints, not output templates. Sub-categories and items emerge from the rules retrieved for the specific pleading in the specific jurisdiction.
